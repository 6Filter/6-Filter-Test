# Competitive Analysis

## 1. Financial teardown of any competitor

1. **Listed** → annual report + investor deck + last eight earnings call transcripts.
   **Unlisted** → company registry filings + credit rating rationale.
2. Extract: revenue, growth, gross margin, marketing spend as % of sales, EBITDA, capacity and utilisation, capex, receivable days (a proxy for distributor health), related-party transactions.
3. **Marketing spend as a share of sales is the most predictive single number** for what a competitor is about to do in your category.
4. Read the auditor's notes and related-party section. That is where the real structure is — captive distributors, contract manufacturing arrangements, promoter entities.
5. Read the risk factors section of any prospectus. Companies are legally obliged to be candid there in a way they never are in a presentation.

## 2. Estimating competitor share without an audit

- Disclosed category revenue ÷ triangulated category size.
- If they do not segment, allocate total revenue across categories using weights: capacity mix, marketing spend mix, SKU count, e-commerce revenue mix. **State the allocation logic explicitly** — it is an assumption, not an observation.
- Cross-check against e-commerce review-velocity share and quick-commerce facing share. Agreement across independent proxies raises confidence; disagreement tells you the brand's channel skew.
- Convert to retail-equivalent before comparing with a retail-value category size. Mixing price levels here is the single commonest error.

## 3. Early-warning dashboard

Build this as a standing weekly job. It is cheap and it repeatedly catches things the organisation would otherwise learn from the trade.

| Signal | Source | Lead time |
|---|---|---|
| New brand or variant coming | Trademark filings by class and applicant | 6–18 months |
| New plant or capacity | Rating rationale, annual report capex, manufacturing licences | 6–24 months |
| Territory expansion | Job postings by city | 3–6 months |
| Campaign launch | Ad transparency libraries | 0–4 weeks |
| Pricing move | Own weekly price panel | 1 week |
| New SKU live | Own weekly assortment panel | 1 week |
| Positioning shift | Web archive snapshots of their site | Continuous |
| Input cost pressure | Commodity price series | 1–3 months |
| Distribution stress | Receivable days in filings; distributor churn on registry | 1–2 quarters |

## 4. The strategy layer

Numbers tell you who is winning. These tell you whether it is durable — which is the question leadership actually needs answered.

**Define competition demand-side, not supply-side.** The real competitive set is what the consumer would use if the product vanished, not the brands sharing an industry category code. A sachet shampoo's competitor may be a bar of soap. Syndicated data will never tell you this; consumer conversations will.

**Separate operational excellence from structural power.** Better advertising, better packaging, better trade schemes are all copyable and therefore not moats. Durable power in consumer goods is: scale economies in media and distribution, genuine brand salience, cornered supply (specific raw material or manufacturing access), and switching costs where they exist. Audit which one the business is actually building — most "advantages" in a competitive deck are the copyable kind.

**Can't versus won't.** For any advantage a rival holds, ask both questions. Is there a structural reason competitors *cannot* replicate it? And would replication require them to damage their existing business, so that they *won't*? The "won't" cases are the openings: a prestige player who cannot come down to mass channels without enraging their existing trade; a distributor-heavy incumbent who cannot move fast into a channel that cannibalises their own network; a premium brand that cannot launch a value line without undermining its price architecture.

**Treat incumbent launches as market signals** even when the launch itself fails. A large player entering a niche tells you where the category is heading and when a niche is about to be commoditised.

**Avoid copying features or formats reactively.** What a competitor ships is the visible output of research and strategy from a year earlier. Reacting to it puts you permanently a year behind on someone else's thinking. Check competitors on a fixed cadence rather than continuously, and anchor decisions to your own consumer understanding.

## 5. Benchmarking set

- **Financial:** gross margin, marketing spend %, EBITDA, working capital, ROCE vs the listed comparator set.
- **Price architecture:** price per standard unit across every competitor SKU, plotted against pack size. Reveals price-pack gaps immediately and generates launch ideas faster than any other single analysis.
- **Portfolio:** SKU count, pack ladder, variant depth, premium/mass mix.
- **Distribution:** direct reach and stockist counts — large players disclose these in investor decks.
- **Marketing:** share of voice via ad library counts, video views, search interest.
- **International:** per-capita consumption versus comparable markets, for headroom.
